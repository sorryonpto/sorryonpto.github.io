First-timer’s guide to Japan: 10 days, Tokyo to Kyoto

By Vania Quach, Fora Travel advisor · 8 min read

Your first trip to Japan should feel exciting, not like a logistics exam. Here’s how I’d pace 10 days from Tokyo to Kyoto, plus the little things I wish everyone knew before landing.

The 10-day route at a glance

  1. Tokyo (3 nights): neighborhoods, food and a little chaos.
  2. Lake Kawaguchiko (1–2 nights): lake views, an onsen, and a chance to see Mt Fuji.
  3. Kyoto (3 nights): temples, gardens and slow mornings.
  4. Osaka (1 night or a day trip): eat everything.

Four bases in 10 days is plenty. If you’d rather unpack less, skip a night somewhere and give Tokyo or Kyoto the extra time. For a full day-by-day version, see my Japan travel advisor page.

What to book before you go

  • Hotels first. Location matters more than you’d think, especially in Tokyo. Cherry blossom and autumn leaf season fill early.
  • The restaurants you’d be heartbroken to miss. Popular spots can book up well in advance, and some take reservations only through specific channels.
  • Timed-entry attractions. Some museums and immersive experiences sell timed tickets ahead and can sell out.
  • A stay with a Fuji view if seeing the mountain matters to you, plus a back-up plan for cloudy days.

Getting around

Trains make Japan easy once you get the rhythm. The bullet train (shinkansen) connects Tokyo and Kyoto in a little over two hours, and Kyoto to Osaka is quick. In cities, a rechargeable IC card such as Suica or PASMO—or the version in your phone’s wallet—lets you tap through trains, buses and many convenience stores.

Before you buy a rail pass, do the math for your exact route. For many first-timer itineraries, individual tickets can make more sense. This is one of the things I check for clients.

Pro tip: Ask your hotel about luggage forwarding. Sending your big suitcase ahead to your next hotel means you can ride the train with just a day bag—one of my favorite Japan conveniences.

Little things that make a big difference

  • Carry some cash. Cards are common, but smaller restaurants, shrines and shops may be cash-only.
  • Tipping isn’t expected. Great service is the norm.
  • Wear shoes that slip off easily. You may remove them at some temples, restaurants and traditional inns.
  • Pack light. Trains, stairs and compact hotel rooms are easier with a smaller suitcase. Plus: more room for shopping.
  • Go early. Popular Kyoto sights are much calmer first thing in the morning.
  • Leave white space. Some of my favorite moments came from wandering into a side street with no plan.

How to pace a first trip

Pick one or two anchors per day, then let the rest happen. Mix big sights with something fun: in Tokyo, I went go-karting through the streets in a costume, and it was one of the highlights. In Kawaguchiko, rent a bike and let the weather decide when you look for Fuji. In Kyoto, plan an unhurried tea break. You don’t need to see everything to fall for Japan.
